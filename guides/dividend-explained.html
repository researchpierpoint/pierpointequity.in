Dividends Explained

Understand declared dividend, ex-dividend date, record date, payment date and dividend yield.

A dividend is a distribution to eligible shareholders under the company's declared terms.

Key dates

Ex-date and record-date rules determine eligibility; payment occurs according to the declared timetable.

Yield

Dividend yield is price-dependent and can rise because a share price falls.

Sustainability

Compare dividends with earnings, cash flow, debt and future capital needs.

Dividend is a capital-allocation decision

A dividend transfers cash from the company to shareholders. The important question is whether the company has better uses for that capital—reinvestment, debt reduction, acquisitions, buybacks or distribution.

Yield vs sustainability

Dividend yield is dividend per share divided by share price. A high yield can arise because dividends are strong or because the share price has fallen. Check payout capacity and cash generation.

Payout ratio

Compare dividends with earnings and, where relevant, free cash flow. A payout ratio can look comfortable against accounting profit while cash generation is weak.

Growth matters too

A modest current yield with durable dividend growth can have different economics from a high yield that is vulnerable to a cut.

Dividend checklist

  • ☐ Yield calculated from current data
  • ☐ Payout ratio checked
  • ☐ Free cash flow checked
  • ☐ Debt obligations considered
  • ☐ Dividend history reviewed
