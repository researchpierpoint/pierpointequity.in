How to Read a Cash Flow Statement

A practical guide to operating, investing and financing cash flows and the questions each section answers.

The cash-flow statement explains how cash moved during a period and complements the income statement and balance sheet.

Operating cash flow

Look at cash generated by the core business and compare it with reported profit.

Investing cash flow

Capital expenditure, acquisitions and asset sales appear here. Large investment can be healthy or risky depending on expected returns.

Financing cash flow

Debt issuance/repayment, dividends, buybacks and equity issuance reveal how the company funds itself.

Red flags

Persistent negative CFO, rising receivables, heavy borrowing to fund operations or repeated equity issuance deserve investigation.
