PEG Ratio Explained

Understand the price/earnings-to-growth ratio, its calculation and why it should not be used mechanically.

PEG attempts to relate a P/E multiple to an earnings-growth rate. It can add context to growth stocks but depends heavily on the growth estimate.

Formula

A simplified PEG is P/E divided by expected EPS growth. Different conventions can use historical or forecast growth and percentages rather than decimals.

Why it can help

A high P/E may be more understandable when sustainable growth is also high. PEG provides a quick way to combine the two variables.

Limitations

Growth quality, duration, cyclicality, debt, margins and capital requirements are not captured by a single PEG number.

Best use

Treat PEG as a screening aid and then examine the underlying earnings assumptions and valuation history.
