Revenue Growth Explained

Learn how to analyse sales growth, volume, price, mix, acquisitions and the quality of reported revenue.

Revenue is the starting point for most operating analysis, but fast sales growth does not automatically mean a better business.

Volume and price

Separate growth caused by higher volumes from price increases, currency effects and product mix where the company provides enough disclosure.

Organic versus acquired

Acquisition-driven growth should be analysed separately from organic growth because integration and future returns can differ.

Receivables

Compare revenue growth with receivables and operating cash flow. A widening gap may require investigation.

Sustainability

Check capacity, market size, competition, customer concentration and capital required to support future growth.

Deep research: decompose revenue growth

Revenue can rise through volume, price, mix, acquisitions, currency or changes in accounting scope. Understanding the bridge is more informative than quoting CAGR alone.

Volume vs price

Price-led growth can increase revenue while volumes stagnate. Volume-led growth can indicate expanding demand but may require capacity and working capital.

Organic vs inorganic

Separate existing-business growth from acquisitions. Evaluate acquired revenue alongside the capital paid and returns generated.

Revenue quality

Check customer concentration, recurring vs transactional revenue, order backlog where relevant, churn, renewal rates and geographic exposure.

Revenue and cash

Fast revenue growth with rapidly rising receivables deserves investigation. Revenue is not equivalent to cash collected.

Revenue-growth checklist

  • ☐ Volume/price/mix understood
  • ☐ Organic growth separated
  • ☐ Customer concentration checked
  • ☐ Receivables tested
  • ☐ Capacity requirements understood
