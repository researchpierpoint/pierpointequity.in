VALUATION GUIDE

What is P/E ratio?

P/E, or price-to-earnings ratio, compares a company's share price with earnings per share. It is widely used, but it is not a complete valuation model.

Formula

P/E = Share price ÷ Earnings per share (EPS)

For example, if a share trades at ₹1,000 and annual EPS is ₹50, the P/E is 20×. The calculation is simple; interpreting the multiple is not.

Trailing versus forward P/E

Trailing P/E uses historical earnings. Forward P/E uses an estimate of future earnings. Forward P/E therefore includes forecast uncertainty and should not be treated as an observed fact.

Why P/E differs between companies

Growth expectations, margins, capital intensity, cyclicality, balance-sheet risk and business quality can all affect the multiple investors are willing to pay.

Useful comparisons

Compare a company's current P/E with its own historical range and with genuinely comparable businesses. A cross-sector comparison can be misleading when economics differ materially.

Important limitations

P/E can be unhelpful when earnings are negative, unusually cyclical or distorted by one-off items. It also ignores the amount of debt used to generate earnings. In those cases, other measures may provide additional context.

Questions to ask

  • Are current earnings representative?
  • What growth assumptions are embedded in the valuation?
  • How does the current multiple compare with the company's history?
  • What would happen to the valuation if earnings disappoint?
