What is ROCE?

Return on capital employed (ROCE) is a profitability measure that relates operating profit to the capital employed in a business.

Why investors use ROCE

ROCE can help answer a basic question: how effectively does a company use the capital tied up in its operations to generate operating returns?

Formula

A common formulation is ROCE = EBIT ÷ Capital employed × 100. Exact definitions vary, so comparisons should use consistent methodology.

Look at the trend

A single year's ROCE can be distorted by the business cycle, acquisitions, asset sales or temporary working-capital changes. A multi-year trend is usually more informative.

Compare with the business model

Capital-intensive industries naturally require more assets and can have different return profiles from asset-light businesses. Peer comparison is therefore important.

ROCE is not a standalone verdict

A high ROCE does not by itself establish that a company is attractively valued or that future returns will match the past. Consider growth, cash conversion, balance-sheet strength, competitive position and valuation alongside it.

Research checklist

  • Is ROCE improving, stable or declining?
  • What is driving the change?
  • How does it compare with relevant peers?
  • Does cash generation support reported profitability?
