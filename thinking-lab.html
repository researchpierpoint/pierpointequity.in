THE CORE LOOP

Five moves that make thinking harder to fool.

Use the same sequence for a market concept, a fictional company case or a research question.

01 · DECOMPOSE

What exactly is the claim?

Rewrite vague language into a statement that could be supported, challenged or measured.

02 · EXPLAIN

What would have to be true?

List the mechanism, assumptions and links between cause and outcome.

03 · VERIFY

What evidence would discriminate?

Separate evidence that merely sounds supportive from evidence that can distinguish competing explanations.

04 · RED-TEAM

What breaks the idea?

Construct the strongest reasonable counter-case before deciding how much confidence the claim deserves.

05 · UPDATE

What would change your mind?

Write the disconfirming evidence in advance. Then return later and compare the record with reality.

06 · TRANSFER

Does it work elsewhere?

Move the concept into another market and identify which parts are universal and which depend on local rules or structure.

PRACTICE FRAMEWORK

Build a thesis without pretending certainty.

A useful research conclusion can contain confidence, uncertainty, assumptions and unresolved questions at the same time.

CLAIM

What do you believe?

Write one sentence. No jargon. No conclusion hidden inside five different claims.

MECHANISM

Why should it happen?

Draw the chain: driver → behaviour → financial or market effect → observable evidence.

FAILURE

How could you be wrong?

List alternative explanations, missing information and the earliest warning signs.

SCENARIO THINKING

Replace one-point certainty with a range of possibilities.

Practice with fictional cases. The objective is not to predict the future; it is to understand how assumptions change outcomes.

Base caseWhat happens if the main assumptions broadly hold?
Upside caseWhich specific assumptions improve, and why?
Downside caseWhich assumption fails first, and what follows?
Disconfirming evidenceWhat observation would force a meaningful update?
